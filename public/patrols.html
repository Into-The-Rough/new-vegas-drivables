Author guide

Patrols on the roads the mod already has. New roads cannot be added from a plugin yet.

For mod authors

Put your own patrol on the road.

A patrol is a quest in your own plugin. You choose who rides, what they ride and where they are heading. The mod finds a road, puts them on it near the player and drives them there. No DLL work and no new models.

What a patrol is

Every group the mod puts on the road, from the Crimson Caravan to the NCR patrol jeep, is built the same way. Yours is no different.

Three parts

  • A quest with a script. The script declares a handful of variables by name and the mod reads them.
  • Pools: a list of drivers, a list of vehicles and a list of the places the patrol travels to.
  • The DrvEncounters form list. A quest on that list is one the mod will consider.

What the mod does with it

  • Picks a road that ends at one of your destinations and admits every vehicle in the group.
  • Places the group on that road out of the player’s sight and within reach.
  • Drives it along the road, round obstacles and past other traffic, to the far end.
  • Retires it once the player has left it behind.

Riders only exist near the player. A patrol is something you meet on the road, not a convoy that crosses the map while you are elsewhere.

Your first patrol

The example is an NCR supply run: one army jeep with a driver and a couple of troopers, travelling between NCR places. Your plugin needs DrivablesNVSE.esm as a master.

  1. Make the pools

    Create three form lists in your plugin. Drivers: NPC bases or a levelled NPC list. Vehicles: vehicle bases from the mod, such as DrvJeepArmy or DrvMotorcycle. Destinations: the map marker references the patrol should travel to, taken from the road table. Repeat an entry in a list to make it more likely.

  2. Write the quest script

    Declare the variables the mod reads and set them once. The last three declared, fLastSpawn, iAlive and rLastLeader, are the mod’s own: declare them and never set them. The block after the set-up decides when the patrol may appear. The script on the right is complete.

  3. Create the quest

    Tick Start Game Enabled, attach the script and give it a short script delay. The shipped patrols use 0.25 seconds.

  4. Put it on the list

    The script adds its own quest to DrvEncounters. The mod reads that list when a save loads, so after installing your plugin, save and load once. Do not edit DrvEncounters in your plugin: two mods that both override the list would cancel each other out.

  5. Test it

    Stand near one of your roads and enter DrvStartEncounter MySupplyRun in the console. It works as soon as the quest is running, before the save and load. The patrol starts if it can. If it cannot, the corner message and drivables_nvse.log say why.

The whole script

scn MySupplyRunScript

ref rDrivers
ref rVehicles
ref rDestinations
ref rPassengers
int iPassengers
int iCount
int iMaxAlive
int iPlacement
int iCategory
int iSizing
int iGate
int iInit
float fCooldown
float fWeight
float fDensity
float fHaltSeconds

float fLastSpawn
int iAlive
ref rLastLeader

Begin GameMode
	if iInit == 0
		set iInit to 1
		set rDrivers to MySupplyDrivers
		set rVehicles to MySupplyVehicles
		set rDestinations to MySupplyDestinations
		set rPassengers to MySupplyTroopers
		set iPassengers to 2
		set iCount to 1
		set iMaxAlive to 1
		set iPlacement to 6
		set iCategory to 2
		set iSizing to 1
		set fCooldown to 900
		set fWeight to 0.6
		set fHaltSeconds to 60
		AddFormToFormList DrvEncounters MySupplyRun
	endif

	if Player.GetDistance MojaveOutpostMapMarker < 30000 || Player.GetDistance PrimmMapMarker < 30000
		set iGate to 1
	else
		set iGate to 0
	endif
	set fDensity to 1
End

The variables

The mod finds each one by its name, so spell them exactly. Declare only the ones you use. A variable you leave out takes its default.

VariableTypeWhat it sets
rDriversform list or levelled NPCWho drives the lead vehicle.
rVehiclesform list or levelled listWhat the lead vehicle can be. The mod only uses entries the chosen road admits.
rDestinationsform list of map markersWhere the patrol travels to. It appears on a road that ends at one of them.
iPlacementintSet to 6 for a road patrol.
iCategoryint2 for a group, which takes the one group slot near the player and waits out the interval between convoys. 0 for a lone rider counted with ordinary traffic.
iCountintVehicles in the group, 1 to 4.
iSizingintHow iCount is used. 0 draws a size up to it, 1 brings exactly that many, 2 brings any number from one to it.
iMaxAliveintHow many of this patrol’s riders may exist at once.
fCooldownfloatSeconds of play between spawns. Waiting, sleeping and fast travel run it down.
fWeightfloatIts chance against the other groups that could appear. The shipped patrols use 0.5 to 0.6.
fDensityfloatSet to 1. Lower it from your script to thin the patrol out, as the Crimson Caravan does when its owner dies.
iGateintSet to 1 while the patrol may appear and 0 while it may not. Your script decides: place, quest stage, reputation, time of day.
fHaltSecondsfloatSet it and the group halts at each end of its road, stands watch for about that long, then turns round and rides back.
rEscorts, rEscortDriversform listsVehicles and drivers for the rest of the group. Without them the followers come from the lead pools.
rLeaders, fLeaderChanceform list, floatA different lead vehicle at that chance, as the Legion patrol’s chariot.
rPassengers, iPassengersform list, intCrew for the lead vehicle’s spare seats, and how many.
rTrailers, fTrailerChanceform list, floatA trailer or cart behind the lead vehicle at that chance.
iInitintYour own flag so the set-up block runs once.

What the mod writes back

VariableTypeHolds
fLastSpawnfloatGameDaysPassed at the last spawn.
iAliveintHow many of the patrol’s riders exist now.
rLastLeaderrefThe driver who led the last group.

Declare all three even if you never read them: the cooldown is kept in fLastSpawn and does nothing without it. They save with the quest, so a cooldown survives a reload. The Drv:OnEncounterSpawn and Drv:OnEncounterEnd events on the Modders page report the same moments.

What each road admits

Every vehicle in the mod has a road class. Horses, bicycles, motorcycles, cars and the rest ride all 21 roads. The heavy trucks ride none of them at present: the switchbacks, ditches and tight turns on these roads have not been proven for a truck’s turning circle. A truck in your vehicle list is simply never picked.

What it will not do

Better to know before you build around it.

  • No new roads from a plugin. The 21 roads are generated from the game’s road surfaces with our own tools. Camp McCarran, the Strip and Freeside are not on any of them.
  • No timetable. A cooldown is the nearest thing to a schedule, and a patrol only ever appears near the player.
  • No fights started for you. Riders whose factions would fight the people where the patrol would appear are not placed there.
  • One group at a time. A group shares the roads with the mod’s own convoys and waits its turn after them.
  • Four vehicles at most in a group.
  • The riders are ordinary NPCs. Their factions, weapons and packages decide what they do when they meet trouble.

When it does not appear

Open drivables_nvse.log in the game directory or your mod manager’s overwrite output and search for your quest’s editor ID. Each attempt says what held it.

My quest is never mentioned in the log

It is not on the list yet. Check that your script ran AddFormToFormList DrvEncounters, then save and load. The line encounter: DrvEncounters holds N quests should count one more than before.

“gate closed”

Your script has iGate at 0. Check the conditions that set it and that the quest is running.

“cooldown” or “convoy interval”

It is waiting its turn. The first is your own fCooldown. The second is the gap the mod keeps between any two groups. DrvStartEncounter skips both for a test.

“No patrol road within reach” or “No patrol road point within reach is free”

None of your destinations is the end of a road near the player, or every point on those roads is in view, taken or too close to its end. Move away from town, look elsewhere and try again. The log line counts each reason.

The wrong vehicle turns up, or never the one I listed

The road does not admit it. Check its class against what each road admits.

The patrol appears and attacks, or is attacked

That is its drivers’ factions at work. Use NPC bases whose factions fit the roads you send them down.
