Installation & requirements

The public download is forthcoming. This is the intended setup, with the final dependency versions and release-specific instructions still to come.

Before installing

  • Fallout: New Vegas on PC with xNVSE installed and working.
  • Use the mod’s full release package, including its plugin, configuration and assets. The DLL alone is not the complete mod.
  • JIP LN NVSE supports the active horse map marker. The final requirements list will be on the Nexus download page.

Developed and tested with Viva New Vegas in mind. TTW is supported, controllers are supported, and the mod has been tested on Steam Deck. The Frontier is not required.

When the download is available

  1. Check the Nexus requirements and installation notes ↗ and install the listed dependencies.
  2. Install the complete archive with your mod manager. Enable both the mod and DrivablesNVSE.esp.
  3. Start the game with xNVSE through your usual mod manager setup. You can use an existing playthrough.
  4. Load a save and press F10, or click both controller sticks together. The Drivables menu should open. Check that world placements are enabled in its settings.

If the menu does not appear, check that xNVSE loaded and the complete mod is enabled. Final troubleshooting instructions will accompany the release.

Your first ride

You can start with a bicycle and a stretch of road. The races, aircraft and more unusual rides can wait until you feel like trying them.

  1. Find something to ride

    With world placements enabled in F10 preferences, vehicles and wild horses appear around the world. Some vehicles work immediately, others need repair, and a few are quest or race rewards.

    Use the placement map for directions. Switch off map details to hide its directions and unlock information. Other guides include spoilers.

  2. Check what it needs

    Inspect a wreck or covered vehicle for its requirements. Bring the listed parts and meet its Repair requirement. Required repair tools and components are consumed when you use them.

    A bicycle, skateboard, Pogo Stick or Hopper needs no fuel. Powered rides use the supply shown in their entry. Fuel and repair guide →

  3. Get comfortable

    Mount your ride and take a short trip somewhere open. Learn how it accelerates, turns and stops before trying a boost or a stunt. F10 → Controls shows the bindings and riding tips.

    Tap Camera to try another view. If the camera moves more than you like, reduce camera motion in F10. Bring a companion along if there is a passenger position.

    Your first ride asks “Tips as you go?”. Choose Show me tips and short cards appear as situations come up, one at a time, until you have used each control. F10 → Controls → Tips lists them and can switch them off or reset them.

  4. Choose what comes next

    Keep exploring, look for another vehicle, try a race or head to the Thunderdome. Prefer horses? Start with a wild horse that has no Survival requirement.

    You can use the guides below as you need them.

F10 is your starting point

The garage, controls, vehicle settings, horses and race setup live in the same menu. On a controller, click both sticks together to open it.

The Garage lists every ride by family. Enter spawns the selected vehicle in front of you, T opens its tuning workshop and V opens the showroom with its records and logbook. Read the F10 menu guide →

A few useful things to know

Fuel is a consumable. Boost charge recovers. Bicycles spend AP to sprint, while horses have their own stamina.

A vehicle’s passenger count excludes you. Solo rides still appear in the catalogue.

For a stuck or overturned vehicle, look for Recover in the garage.

Driving & riding

Choose your ride and input device for the default controls. Keyboard, Xbox and Steam Deck layouts are available. Your own bindings may differ, so check F10 → Controls in game.

Default bindings. Bright buttons are used by this ride.

    Lights, ignition & radio
      Click both sticks together to open the F10 menu.

      LS and RS mean move the left or right stick. L3 and R3 mean click it. View is the small left menu button, also called Back. A, B, X and Y follow the stock Fallout control layout where shared. Your game bindings, pad overrides or Steam Input layout may change them. Check F10 → Controls for your current buttons.

      Boost & sprint

      On rides with boost enabled, hold Boost while accelerating. Boost spends a charge that recovers when you stop using it. If you empty it, let it recover before boosting again.

      Boost adds power rather than fixing every vehicle at the same extra speed. Fuel-using vehicles consume 1.5 times their normal fuel rate while boosting.

      The same action makes a bicycle sprint using your AP. On a horse, tap it to increase gait or hold it to sprint using the horse’s stamina. The Alien Recon Craft has its own warp boost and still needs no fuel.

      Hover Bike & repulsor

      Throttle and Brake control forward movement. Steer normally, double-tap left or right to dodge sideways, and press Jump for a short hop. Holding Jump does not turn the bike into an aircraft. Settle back down before hopping again.

      Handbrake slows the bike and prevents boost. It has no default controller button, so use the brake trigger to slow down on a pad. Use the Horn / Repulsor action to fire an outward pulse that pushes nearby actors away. The standard cooldown is two seconds.

      The pulse respects solid obstructions and excludes your companions. It can provoke people you hit. Tune the bike’s hop and hover behaviour in its F10 vehicle settings.

      Flying

      Propeller aircraft

      Use Throttle and Brake on the ground. Build speed along a clear take-off run, then use the mouse or flight stick to pitch and bank. Rudder left / right adjusts your heading. Landing Gear changes the gear and Cruise holds altitude and heading. On a controller, the left stick pitches and banks while the right stick looks around. With a mouse, hold Aim for free look.

      On the ground the HUD surveys the run ahead and reports whether it looks clear, rough, blocked or too short, then cues PULL BACK NOW at rotation speed. In the air it shows knots, an artificial horizon, height above ground, gear lamps, a bar per engine and a flight path marker.

      Cruise needs steady powered flight above about 27 m/s with 12 m of clearance. Steering or braking cancels it. Holding Brake in the air works as an air brake.

      Line up early for landing, lower the gear, reduce speed and keep the approach shallow. A gear-down approach gets a little landing help near the ground. A gentle gear-up touchdown on flat ground becomes a belly slide. A large aircraft needs far more room than the Stunt Biplane.

      Engines take damage one at a time. A damaged engine loses power, smokes and coughs, then fails and windmills, and the plane yaws towards the dead side. The Small Prop Plane can lose a wing, which is your cue to bail out. The B-29 has four engines to lose but keeps its wings. Fly to the edge of the map and the plane turns itself round.

      Rolls, loops and inverted flight show a card with your speed in knots, and the Stunt Biplane is built for them.

      Vertibird & Alien Recon Craft

      Hold Climb to rise and Descend to lower the craft. Release both and the Vertibird holds its height, shown as HEIGHT HOLD or MANUAL LIFT. Use thrust and steering to travel. The Hover Bike has its separate hop controls.

      The Vertibird’s gear key cycles Auto, Down and Up. Auto retracts in a climb and lowers on a slow approach. Its Lights key is a searchlight with three states: Aim follows where you look, Hold keeps it on the spot, then Off. The HUD shows a drift dial, height, climb rate, gear lamps, fuel and hull condition, with warnings such as SINK RATE, GEAR UP, SLOPE TOO STEEP and LANDING OBSTRUCTED. A low hover raises dust or spray and can knock a bystander off their feet without hurting them.

      Weapons, payloads, thermal view and flares are in the combat guide. Exit bails out. Know how to deploy the parachute before jumping.

      Parachutes

      1. Board an aircraft that supplies a chute. Look for “Parachute packed”. The Vertibird, Small Prop Plane, B-29, Stunt Biplane and Alien Recon Craft supply one.
      2. Use Exit to bail out. You can steer in freefall, and holding Run or Sneak dives head-down to fall faster. Release Exit, then press Activate / Exit again while falling to deploy.
      3. Steer under the canopy with the movement controls. Crouch gives a faster descent.
      4. Near the ground, hold Backward to flare and brake your landing. The HUD shows FLARE.

      Press Activate / Exit again to cut away. Cutting away sends you tumbling at full gravity, and falling without a chute is lethal. A chute is used once, then replenished when you ride a supplying aircraft again.

      Companions wait for you to deploy before opening their canopies. Once deployed, theirs remain open until they land, even if you cut yours away or touch down first.

      Skateboard, Pogo Stick & Hopper

      Skateboard

      Forward kicks to build speed, Backward uses the foot brake, and steering shifts your direction. Jump performs an ollie. Boost + Jump performs a kickflip. Hold Handbrake for a manual, but holding it too long can throw you off.

      Pogo Stick & Hopper

      Use Forward to keep bouncing and steer to change direction. Hold Jump while grounded to charge a higher jump, then release. Both rides use bounce controls and need no fuel.

      See the stunt controls for each ride →

      Everyday controls

      Ignition starts or stops the engine. A running engine is loud, so attackers hear you coming. Tap Camera to change view. Every ride starts in third person and restores your own view when you get off.

      Horn

      Motor horns sound for as long as you hold the key. The bicycle has a bell, the H1 has a three-note airhorn and the tanks have siren horns. On the Patrol Car the same key switches the siren and roof beacons on and off. On the Hover Bike it fires the repulsor, and on the Alien Recon Craft holding it works the tractor beam.

      Up to two nearby people notice a horn and say something about it, from a glance to a complaint. It never starts a fight or changes detection. Settings → Driving → Horn NPC reactions turns the barks off.

      Lights

      Headlights come on by themselves indoors and from sunset to sunrise. Pressing Lights yourself overrides that until you get off. Settings → Driving → Automatic headlights disables the automatic part. Cars have working brake and reversing lamps, and the Motorcycle, Motor Chariot and Formula Racer show red-hot brake discs after hard braking that cool in the airflow.

      Radio

      Almost every motorised ride has a radio: all the cars, the Motorcycle, Hover Bike, boat, aircraft, tanks and APC. Radio Power switches it on or off and Next Station steps through the stations in range, using the Pip-Boy’s own reception rules. Each vehicle remembers its station for the session and switches off when you get out.

      Dashboard, map & compass

      The road dashboard shows speed, revs, gear, fuel, condition and warning lamps, plus an ammo readout when you are armed. Many cars also have working cockpit gauges. Settings → Display → Road dashboard chooses Full, Compact or Off, and Speed units picks mph or km/h. Aircraft always read in knots. A heading strip in the bottom-left corner replaces the game compass on motorised rides, and Settings → Bodywork → Heading strip hides it.

      In aircraft and tanks the Pip-Boy key opens a north-up map overlay with your position while you keep control. On other rides the Pip-Boy opens instantly, without the arm coming up.

      Weapons

      Where riding with a weapon is supported, Weapon Toggle draws it. On a controller, the bumpers become Aim and Fire. Tap Reload to reload, or hold it to holster. On the keyboard, Reload holsters while riding. Which rides take which weapons, and what the tanks and aircraft can do, is in the combat guide.

      Camera

      Settings → Camera holds Camera motion (Full, Reduced or Off), Speed FOV, Chase pullback, Crash director, which frames a big crash like a film, and Road and speed shake. Settings → Effects holds Collision effects, Dynamic FOV, Camera tilt / roll, Jump slow motion and the Missile camera chance. Each vehicle also has its own chase distance, camera height and pullback under Garage → Tuning → Camera.

      To adjust the view while riding, press Numpad * or hold both sticks for half a second. Numpad 8 and 2 move the camera up and down, 4 and 6 move it in and out, and Numpad 0 resets it. On a pad the D-pad adjusts and Y resets. The trim is saved per vehicle.

      Handling & recovery

      Settings → Driving → Handling preset chooses Arcade, Balanced or Sim assistance across the fleet, with a Lateral grip slider beside it. Rider load slows a ride and dulls its steering once you carry more than about 150 lb, and at 1,000 lb it refuses you. Crippled arms make steering harder when Rider injuries is on. Power armour cannot ride at all.

      Recover, also available in the F10 garage, rights a stuck or overturned vehicle. It only works while you are aboard, stopped and settled on the ground, with a three-second cooldown. It is not available in aircraft, and a boat has to reverse off the bank instead. In a race it only rights an overturned car. If you are thrown clear, a marker floats over your ride until you get back to it.

      Pad buttons can be rebound per ride under F10 → Controls → Keys, and keyboard keys under Settings → Drive keys, Other keys, More keys and Aircraft. Check your supplies before leaving or entering a race.

      Take your companions along

      Nearby eligible companions board available passenger positions when you enter a vehicle, then get out with you. The counts below exclude the driver. A physical seat on the model does not always have a passenger position.

      Seats, pillions & crew

      The Motorcycle and Hover Bike have pillion positions. Larger cars and trucks can take several passengers. Some vehicles support Rex or another dog in a dedicated position. Robots, animals and unusual companion bodies do not fit every seat.

      The Pershing and Commonwealth Tank carry an enclosed crew member. Their optional Require companion gunner setting makes the main cannon need a living human or ghoul in that position.

      Seated companions holster their weapons and do not fire from a vehicle. Enemies may still shoot at an open cab.

      Companions on horseback

      When you mount a horse outdoors outside a race, eligible humanoid companions receive temporary horses and follow alongside your journey. They match your pace, can dismount for combat and dismount when you do.

      You do not need to tame a separate horse for each companion. Waiting companions, Rex, ED-E and Lily do not use these humanoid rider positions. When you tether your horse at a hitching post, their horses tether at the same rail and leave when you untether. Read the horse guide →

      Shooting from a ride

      You can fire from cars, motorbikes, the bicycle, unicycle and wheelchair, the Hopper and Pogo Stick, the Hover Bike and horses. The skateboard, boat, aircraft, tanks and APC do not allow a personal weapon.

      What you can draw
      RideAllowedRefused
      Cars, bikes, oddballs, Hover BikeOne-handed pistolsEverything else, with the message “Pistols only on this ride”
      HorsesOne-handed melee, pistols, rifles and automatic riflesLaunchers, miniguns, flamers and two-handed melee

      Fire draws an allowed weapon straight away. Weapon Toggle draws or holsters, and if the weapon in your hands is not allowed it swaps to the best one that is, preferring a loaded gun and then the highest damage. With a weapon out, the mouse wheel cycles through your allowed weapons. The clip reloads itself when it runs dry, and holding Aim steadies you for a tighter spread.

      On bikes and horses you cannot fire more than 90 degrees to either side, and the trigger clicks when you try. In an enclosed cab, enemies shoot the vehicle rather than you, and gunfire wears down its condition. In an open cab or on an exposed ride they aim at you.

      With a one-handed melee weapon drawn on a horse, Fire swings to the side you are looking, with half again the normal reach. The horse itself can kick, which is covered in the horse guide. Shooting, burning or puncturing a vehicle that someone else is driving counts as attacking the driver, with the usual consequences.

      Being knocked off

      Hard impacts and lost balance throw you from an exposed ride. A powered Hover Bike absorbs the impacts that would eject you from a bike. If you are crawling along below about 8 mph on a bike, board or bouncer, a hostile within reach can shove you off, and the Hopper can be shot and popped from under you.

      While you are down, Activate gets you up faster. A marker floats over the ride you left until you climb back on or walk up to it, and the F10 Garage’s Recover brings it to you if it has gone somewhere awkward.

      Tanks: turret, sights & secondary weapons

      The turret follows your aim at a steady slew rate and the main gun takes four seconds to reload, with a loader sound when the next round is in. The Commonwealth Tank’s turret turns 120 degrees to either side and crosses the front. The Pershing turns all the way round. The HUD shows a turret rose, a reload bar and a missile lamp.

      In first person you look through a periscope with a limited arc, and tapping Camera takes you outside. Holding Aim in first person gives the gunner’s sight, with a range ladder and a ring that fills as the gun reloads. The APC has the periscope only.

      Pershing: Payload fires a guided missile salvo. Your aim has to be within 30 degrees of the barrel, with three seconds between salvos. The HUD says WAIT or ARC when it refuses.

      Commonwealth Tank: hold Payload for the flame projector. It burns tank fuel, about four seconds of flame for eight units, and heats up. The FLAME bar turns to COOL when it overheats, so release it and let it cool to a quarter before the next burst.

      Vertibird: nose gun, payloads & thermal view

      Fire works the chin turret, which slews towards your crosshair within 60 degrees of the nose. Garage → Vertibird → Tuning → Weapons chooses the fittings.

      Vertibird fittings
      SettingOptionsNotes
      Vertibird nose gunGatling or PlasmaPlasma fires more slowly and hits harder.
      Vertibird payloadGuided, Rocket pods or Mini-nukesGuided fires two homing missiles with a three-second cooldown. Rocket pods fire six unguided rockets. Mini-nukes drop one from your own inventory, need 36 m of clearance and take twelve seconds to reload.

      Tap Reload to cycle the thermal view through white-hot, black-hot and off. People, engines, fires, explosions and fresh corpses glow, and corpses cool over about a minute. Hold the crosshair on a hostile for half a second to lock on. The lock shows the range, guided missiles take it and lead the target, and a small circle shows where to put the nose gun. Rockets never home. Hold Reload to stow the gun or arm it again.

      The HUD refuses a shot with STOW, GUN when your personal weapon is out, WAIT, ARC, AMMO or LOW. When a guided missile is away, the camera follows it in slow motion. Jump skips the shot, and Settings → Effects → Missile camera chance sets how often it happens.

      Flares and incoming missiles. The Vertibird and the Alien Recon Craft carry flares. Numpad 1 drops three, with a six-second recharge shown as FL WAIT. A missile locked onto you sounds a warning tone, shows INBOUND and slows time once per engagement so you can react. The flare key and the slow-down are set in the INI, not in F10.

      B-29 bomb bay

      Payload drops a mini-nuke from your inventory. You need 36 m of ground clearance and twelve seconds between drops. While you are armed and airborne, a sight pip on the ground shows the predicted impact point with a countdown to it.

      Alien Recon Craft: tractor beam & blaster

      Fire works the nose blaster. Hold Horn to open the tractor beam, which needs the craft to be moving at 6 m/s or less, or you get “Too fast for the beam”. The craft holds station while the view follows the first body up.

      Up to four living people or creatures under the hull are lifted, from a widening cone roughly 23 m deep. After four seconds hanging they are pulled aboard and disintegrate, which counts as your attack. Essential characters hang but are never taken. Companions, mounts and anyone driving are left alone. Let go and everyone drops.

      Race weapons are different: during a race nobody can fire, and the bicycle races have their own kicks and dynamite.

      On the road

      Trailers and carts, the people and props you meet, the traffic that shares the Mojave with you, and what happens to a ride you leave behind.

      Car trailerHorse cartPeople in the roadProps that breakTrafficFast travel & saves

      Towing a trailer

      The Old Rusty Car, Corvega, Flatbed Truck, Highwayman, Patrol Car, Death Car, Interceptor and DeSoto can tow the Military Trailer. One sits outside Camp Forlorn Hope and another on the Camp Golf car park, or spawn one from F10 → Garage. A tow ball appears on the car when a trailer is near. Reverse onto the trailer slowly and nearly straight, and it hitches itself with “Trailer hitched.”

      To unhitch, stop and hold Jump for a moment, or use Unhitch Trailer on the service wheel. There is no hotkey. Yank it hard or jackknife past the limit and “The hitch snapped.” The trailer bed is 500 lb of storage, opened like any other vehicle’s.

      The horse-drawn cart

      There’s a Wooden Cart parked by the water brahmin at the 188 and another at Grub n’ Gulp, or spawn one from the Garage. Back a mounted horse slowly between the shafts, nearly in line, and it harnesses itself with “Cart harnessed.” To unharness, dismount, hold Activate on the horse and pick Unharness Cart from the wheel. A harnessed horse left on its own stands still.

      The cart bed holds 1,500 lb and visible crates and sacks pile up as it fills. The horse feels the weight: from about 400 lb all up it slows evenly, until at 3,000 lb it cannot move at all, and it tires faster the heavier the load. The messages read “Heavy cart: your horse is at N% speed” and “The cart is too heavy for your horse to move.” It never refuses the ride.

      People in the road

      Someone standing in your path steps aside if they see you coming. Contact knocks a person back from about 7 mph and hurts them from about 13 mph. A fast front hit on a closed bonnet can roll the victim up onto it for a short ride. Each vehicle’s logbook counts who you have run over, and a horse’s counts who it has trampled.

      Settings → Damage sets the push speed, the damage speed, run-over damage and run-over force. Racing vehicles pass through living people during a car race, so a stranded companion cannot block the circuit.

      Props that break

      Speed, stop, highway, state and wooden direction signs topple when you hit them. Railway crossing arms snap. Street lamps and single utility poles fall, Goodsprings included, while pole groups and lamps with lights attached stay put. Wooden trestle and boardwalk barriers, crates, wooden carts and drive-in speakers knock over or smash, and a collapsed mailbox can be crushed further and keeps whatever was inside. Joshua trees and yuccas burst into leaves. Toppled props stay down in your save.

      Creosote bushes fold flat under cars and stay flat until you load a save. Light clutter such as cans, buckets, loose tyres and tumbleweeds is knocked aside rather than stopping you. The Pershing, Commonwealth Tank, APC and H1 flatten wrecked cars anywhere in the Mojave and climb over them.

      Traffic, robbing & ride theft

      Once you have left Doc Mitchell’s and finished the Goodsprings tutorials or left town, other riders start appearing on the roads near you, a couple at a time. Most are cyclists, a few are on horseback, and now and then someone passes on a skateboard, unicycle, wheelchair, pogo stick or Hopper. Traffic is set in DrivablesTraffic.ini in the config folder rather than F10.

      Look at a cyclist or boarder, or stand close, and the prompt reads Rob. That shoves them off, counts as assault with the usual consequences, and puts you on the ride. Activating a traffic rider’s horse makes them dismount. Shooting a ride counts as attacking its rider.

      Hostiles return the favour. One within reach can push you off a slow exposed ride, and one can take a ride you have left within twenty seconds, with “Someone’s taken your ride.” Wrecks and bodies left from a fight are cleared after three game days away. Rides you took are never removed.

      Fast travel & saving while riding

      You can fast travel from the Pip-Boy map while riding and arrive still in the seat. A ridden horse comes with you. A save made while riding loads you straight back into the seat, engine running, with no entry animation. Parked vehicles stay where you left them across saves, and the Motorcycle drops its side stand. Horses can also ride through town gates, which is in the horse guide.

      Stunts

      Choose a ride for its tricks and controls. Each trick has a button combination and an explanation using action names. Controller combinations assume the default bindings and a holstered weapon.

      Records & XP

      Open F10 → Controls → Records for personal bests by ride type, in five groups. Speed and air: top speed, airtime, biggest and longest jump, biggest drop, distance and longest ride. Tricks: wheelie, stoppie, manual, kickflips, flips, barrel rolls and spin in one jump. Crashes: biggest crash, most damage, most rolls in one crash and most people run over in one ride. Air: highest altitude, fastest dive, engine-off glide, rolls in a row, loops in one flight, inverted flight and lowest parachute pull. Horses and arena: longest gallop, highest horse jump, trampled, hoop and pylon times, trucks cleared, set pieces in one ride and the crush records. Each vehicle also keeps its own logbook in the showroom.

      With Stunt XP on, a stunt pays up to 50 XP, and only when a card shows on screen. Repeating the same stunt pays half each time, down to a tenth, and never recovers, so try different challenges. A new ride-type record adds 15. Distance milestones of 1, 10, 50 and 100 miles pay 10, 20, 35 and 50. A race podium pays 50, 30 and 20, the hoop course 40, and a pylon lap, the crush line and the car tower 30 each.

      Jump cards & slow motion

      With camera motion on Full and Jump slow motion on, a jump that will last more than about a second drops to half speed for up to a second, once per jump. The card names the trick, from Big air to Double backflip or barrel roll, with distance, airtime and take-off speed, then grades the landing: Perfect, Clean landing, Rough landing, Barely made it or Wipeout. Jumps without slow motion still count for records.

      A loop shows “Loop the loop” with its height, graded Clean, Hung on or Scraped through, and in third person the camera swings to a side-on shot through it. Settings → Showtime → Jump assist chooses Fun, Balanced or Realistic air control for cars. The first time you ride each model, a title card shows its class and tagline, and short music stingers mark big moments.

      Practise in the Thunderdome

      Use the ramps, truck jump, rings of fire, flight hoops and car-crushing courses. Travel through F10 → Racing → Thunderdome. Inside the arena any unlocked vehicle can be delivered from the Garage, whatever the World vehicle spawning setting says.

      The hoop course counts “Hoop N of M” and records your time. The pylon race wants every pylon rounded on the outside, and cutting inside one or taking too long voids the lap. A marker shows the next hoop or pylon and the distance to it. The knife edge, hangar, bridge, wires, ring of fire and wall of fire each give a card with your speed, and a quick repeat chains a x2. The truck jump counts the trucks you cleared.

      Races

      Road racing, buggies, bicycles and horses. Set up a race from anywhere through F10 → Racing → Set up a race.

      Get onto the grid

      1. Choose a course and your ride.
      2. Set 1 to 10 laps and 1 to 7 opponents. Use a random field, the same ride as yours, or a custom roster.
      3. Choose debris and raider levels where the course supports them.
      4. Start the race. Starting on foot provides a loan ride. To use your own, select the ride you are already driving.

      If you start in your own ride, stop behind the finish line facing along the course. The field lines up ahead of you and you start at the back. Travelling through F10 → Racing while seated brings your vehicle there and back. Inside a race world, the freestanding race board at the pits opens setup as well.

      A camera pass runs down the grid before the countdown. Jump or Activate skips it, and Settings → Showtime → Race fly-by removes it. The countdown holds the field in place. Pass checkpoints in order and finish every lap: the active gate is amber and flashes green as you cross it. A tower on the left shows the lap, every place, arrows when places change, and gaps in metres, laps, FINISHED or DNF. A small bar shows how much slipstream you are getting.

      Rules & DNFs

      Nobody in the race world can fire or swing a weapon while a race is on, not you, your companions or the drivers. Vehicle swaps and servicing are blocked. In a car, getting out before the flag, wrecking the car or dying is a DNF. On a bicycle or horse, coming off gives you 25 seconds to get back on. Once your finish is recorded you can get out freely, and after a DNF the rest of the field races on.

      Opponents are drawn from Easy Pete, Sunny Smiles, Chet, Trudy, Doc Mitchell, Boone and Cass, each with a random temperament. Aim at a rival and their name shows above the car. They say their piece when you hit them or pass them. Racing vehicles pass through living people, so a stranded companion cannot block the track, and unoccupied vehicles parked on the course are hidden until the race ends.

      When you have finished or dropped out, “Press Activate to end race” estimates the times of anyone still running and opens Final Standings: place, driver, car, status, time and gap. Activate closes the board. Open F10 during a race for a card with your position, lap and time and the choices Restart, which puts the same field back on the grid, Retire, Return Home and Resume. The world pauses while the card is up.

      Fuel & stamina

      A loan vehicle starts with a full supply. Your own vehicle keeps its fuel level, so refuel before entering. Repairs and refuelling are blocked during the race.

      Running dry and coming to a stop causes a DNF. Bicycles use AP to sprint. Horses use their own stamina, which matters when jumping fences.

      Race settings

      Cars can slipstream behind moving rivals. Bicycles do not receive that benefit. Optional light catch-up helps trailing opponents without slowing the leader.

      Track debris and roadside raiders apply to car races on the Nuka-Cola Speedway and the Mojave Oval only, each at Off, Light, Normal or Heavy. Debris partly blocks the lanes and the opponents plan around it. Raiders are pairs of riflemen beside the road, and the ones you kill respawn with the cell. Load distant road loads the scenery ahead of the field.

      You can change crowd size, crowd volume and countdown or checkpoint sounds in the racing settings.

      Bicycle races: kicks & dynamite

      In a bicycle race, Fire kicks a rider beside you. Steering picks the side, otherwise the camera does, and there are four seconds between kicks. A good one knocks them off, and the same can happen to you.

      Glowing bundles around the Cycle Circuit lap hold one stick of dynamite each. Ride through one to pick it up. Hold Aim to light and aim it, keep holding to cook the fuse, and release to throw, with eight seconds between throws. A close blast knocks riders off, and the opponents throw too.

      Horse races

      Hitting or under-jumping a steeplechase fence plays a thud and “Missed fence! Recovering…”, and your horse is held to a trot for a second and a half. There is no damage and your time is not adjusted.

      Karting

      The Repconn Arena is a go-kart race. The go-kart comes in eight colours with the same handling, has no fuel or boost, tops out at about 56 mph and can be spawned any time from Garage → Oddballs. On foot you are lent the red kart against the other seven colours. If you arrive in a kart you keep its colour.

      About the drive-in

      How it works

      Start on foot or while driving. Your own vehicle stays where you left from, and you arrive at the drive-in with a fully fuelled, repaired buggy. The three-second countdown waits until you are seated. Then survive five minutes. Waves grow every 45 seconds, and the HUD shows the time left, your kills and the wave.

      Ram ghouls to squash them. If you slow down or stop, the ones that reach the buggy claw at it and wear it down, and ten of them will wreck a stationary buggy in well under a minute. Speeding up shakes them off. Kills count towards your score but give no XP.

      The run ends if you lose the buggy or get out of it, shown as BUGGY DESTROYED or LEFT THE BUGGY. Leaving the arena retires it. Dying works as it always does, with the usual reload. The results board offers Go Again, which hands you a repaired, fuelled buggy without restoring your own health, or Go Home. Retire and Go Home are also on the F10 card.

      Horse guide

      Three wild herds with two to four horses each. Bay, black, grey, dapple grey, palomino and white coats can appear at any herd.

      Show herds on the map

      Find & tame

      1. Inspect a wild horse to see its traits and Survival requirement.
      2. Meet the requirement and complete the calming interaction while out of combat. No items are needed.
      3. Mount the tamed horse. While sitting on it, open F10 → Horses → Add ridden horse to register it.

      Taming checks effective Survival, including temporary bonuses. You do not need to repeat the skill check on later rides.

      A horse that belongs to someone else shows Steal and asks you to confirm. Mounting it, or registering it in F10, raises the usual alarm, and that includes the horses of riders you meet on the road.

      Choose a horse

      Speed changes its pace. Endurance reduces stamina use and improves recovery. Nerve helps it resist panic and stay controllable when frightened.

      Traits are rated out of five. Coat colour does not determine ability. Herd 1 guarantees a horse with no skill gate. Herd 3 guarantees a Survival 75 horse.

      Each horse also has a personality. A Curious horse has Speed as its best trait, a Reserved horse has Nerve, and the rest are Steady. It sets how often the horse looks around and grazes while it waits for you.

      TypeSurvivalSpeedEnduranceNerve

      Riding & stamina

      Open the visual control guide and choose Horse. Tap Gait up / Sprint to move into a faster gait. Tap Brake to slow down a gait. Hold Sprint for a burst of speed, Jump to clear an obstacle, and Stop to halt.

      A stamina bar appears whenever stamina is not full or you are above the default gait. At empty the horse is exhausted: it refuses to sprint and holds a canter until it has recovered a quarter of its stamina. Give it time between sprints. Endurance and your bond improve recovery.

      By default the horse turns towards wherever you look while your weapon is holstered. Draw a weapon and you steer with the keys or left stick instead. Settings → Horses → Horse view steering switches the look-steering off. Hold the mapped view control to look around without steering.

      Kick. At a stop or a walk, press Kick (R, or L3 on a pad). On its own it is a double hind kick. With Forward it is a front strike, and with Left or Right it is that side’s hind leg. It hits the nearest person or creature within a couple of metres, knocks them back, and counts as your attack.

      Frights. Unsuppressed gunfire within about 25 m spooks a horse, including your own shots from the saddle. Silenced weapons do not. Explosions and taking damage spook it too. A frightened horse bolts away from the source for a few seconds and you keep between a third and most of your steering, depending on Nerve and bond. A bad fright makes it rear first. A fully bonded Steady horse can ignore gunfire. Reassure calms a mild fright but will not stop a bolt.

      Landings. Come down hard and the horse stumbles to a walk for a moment. Come down very hard and you both go down, you take damage, and you cannot remount until the horse is back on its feet.

      Load. Carry more than about 150 lb and the horse slows, handles worse and tires faster, with “Heavy load: your horse is at N% speed”. At 1,000 lb it refuses you, and it will not carry power armour. Crippled arms make steering harder.

      A ridden horse swims through deep water with you aboard, though you cannot mount while submerged or whistle from the water. Hooves splash in the shallows and puff in snow. People will still greet you and start conversations while you sit on the horse, so there is no need to dismount to talk.

      Stable & whistle

      The first living horse you register becomes active. Adding another horse does not change that selection. Choose Make Active in F10 → Horses, or on the horse’s command wheel, to switch. Riding an unregistered horse does not replace it. You can name horses and remove them from the stable through the same menu. Settings → Horses → Active horse map marker shows the active horse on the world and local maps, which needs JIP LN NVSE and is not a fast-travel point.

      The whistle. On foot, press the horse care key, H on the keyboard or hold D-pad down on a pad. Within a few metres of your active horse it pets it. Farther away it whistles: you holster, give a whistle in first or third person, and the horse answers with a nicker. A horse in the area runs to you. One that is unloaded or elsewhere is brought to a spot behind your view and runs in from there. The call gives up after thirty seconds if the horse cannot reach you.

      The whistle is refused indoors, while the horse is tethered, ridden, frightened or fighting, when either of you is in water, when it is already on its way, or when you have no active horse.

      Feeding, care & trust

      The command wheel. Hold Activate on a horse in your stable for a moment. A tap mounts it instead, and horses outside your stable have no wheel. The eight slots are Make Active, Feed, Follow Me or Wait Here, Ride, Untether or Unharness Cart, Pet or Brush, Saddlebags and Stimpak. The centre shows health, stamina, traits, bond, what the horse is doing and any lame legs.

      Horse Feed restores health and stamina and is consumed when used. A bag or three lies on the ground beside every hitching post, and they turn up again once the cell has respawned. One feed gives 70 health and 35 stamina, and the slot is greyed out when the horse needs neither. A dusty horse offers Brush in place of Pet. On foot, H within a few metres of your active horse walks you to its side to stroke its neck, and a Trusted horse leans into it.

      Riding and meaningful care build your bond through Unfamiliar, Familiar at 10%, Trusted at 50% and Close at 80%. Calm riding earns a little all the time. A pet earns 1% and a feed 1.5%, and the two share a five-minute reward cooldown. Greater trust helps recovery and reduces panic. Hitting your own horse outside a fight costs bond from the second hit and leaves it wary for a minute or two.

      While riding, use Reassure / Pat to calm your horse. It needs third person and a holstered weapon. On the move it is a reach and a stroke, at a standstill a full pat, with eight seconds between uses.

      Lameness and Stimpak. A crippled leg cuts the horse’s power, and mounting tells you which leg (“Your horse is lame in its left foreleg”). Too lame and it refuses to carry you. Stimpak on the wheel uses one from your inventory or the saddlebags, heals the horse and mends every leg. Settings → Damage → Rider injuries controls it.

      Coat. Dust builds on dirt and sand, showing as Light dust and then Dusty, and Brush appears once it starts. Wading wets the legs, then the belly, then the whole horse, and dries in a few minutes, with deep water washing the lower dust off. Rain soaks a horse standing in the open. A long canter or gallop brings out sweat that fades over a few minutes. Horses also leave droppings every few minutes, ridden or not, which disappear on their own.

      Settings → Horses holds Affectionate greetings, a quiet nicker when you come back to a horse that likes you, and Recovery sounds. Settings → Affinity sets whether bond changes show as notifications, at Off, Milestones or Full, with their sound volume and reduced motion.

      Fuel supplies

      Jerry Cans
      Used by liquid-fuel vehicles, including the Vertibird. Petrol, diesel and biofuel share the same supply. Partly used cans are consumed. Bulk refuelling is supported. Four traders keep one in stock at a time, and it isn’t cheap: Old Lady Gibson, Quartermaster Mayes at Forlorn Hope, Dale Barton at the Fort and the Boomers’ munitions manager. A dozen more lie around the petrol stations and yards further down the road, and once they’re gone they’re gone.
      Energy Cells / Microfusion Cells
      Charge the Highwayman, Hover Bike and Camp Golf Cart. One Microfusion Cell supplies twice the charge of one Energy Cell.
      Reactor Coolant
      Used by the nuclear Corvega, both patrol cars, the DeSoto, the Vertibird, the El Dorado street cleaner and the quarry bucket loaders, and all of them turn up with the reservoir dry. One can is a quarter of a reservoir. Knight Torres in the Hidden Valley bunker and the Boomers’ munitions manager keep one each, and I’ve left fourteen lying about: one beside each dead reactor vehicle and the rest at HELIOS One, Black Mountain and Camp Searchlight. They don’t come back either, but a can does turn up now and then in toolboxes and in factory, military and fire house clutter, and you can set how often in the ini.

      Restoring a vehicle

      Inspect a covered vehicle or original wreck to see its repair requirements. Some discoveries only need Repair skill. Others also need one or two Car Repair Tools. The crashed Vertibird needs a Sensor Module.

      Bring restoration parts in your inventory. Required components are consumed when restoration succeeds. Each map entry lists the exact requirements.

      Before a long trip

      Large aircraft need a long, clear take-off run. Check your fuel and leave enough room to land.

      The service wheel

      Stand behind a parked car, a little way past its rear bumper, and the prompt changes from Drive to Service. Activate opens a wheel with Repair, Refuel (or Recharge, or Top up coolant), Storage, Drive, Wash and the Drivables menu. A discovery shows Restore Engine or Install Part here, the APC shows Open Rear Ramp, and a towing car shows Unhitch Trailer. The card in the middle reports condition, fuel, cargo weight, tyres and any body faults.

      Servicing is blocked during a race. On the APC, open the ramp while parked and empty, then crouch through the rear doorway to walk inside. Clear the ramp before closing it.

      Routine repairs

      Repair uses Car Repair Tools. Each box restores 30% of maximum condition plus 0.3% per point of Repair skill, so 30% at Repair 0 and 60% at Repair 100. One Repair action uses up to four boxes, taking from the vehicle’s own storage first and then your inventory, and repairs partially if you are short. Body-only damage costs a single box. There is no caps charge and no skill gate.

      Any successful repair also mends flat tyres, shattered glass, shot-off doors, broken headlamps, open bonnets and boots, lost bumpers and dents. A repair above 15% puts out a burning car.

      Condition, leaks & wrecks

      Every vehicle has a condition percentage. Crashes above about 13 mph, gunfire, melee and explosions lower it. Settings → Bodywork → Crash speed moves that threshold. An engine at 50% or below smokes while it runs, and a liquid-fuel vehicle below 10% leaks, emptying a full tank in about three minutes on top of normal use. Cell-powered rides do not leak.

      Road cars at 15% condition or below catch fire. They burn for an eight-second warning, then explode, and more hits do not restart the timer. Repairing above 15% puts the fire out. An explosion shoves nearby parked cars and rolls them away, and a car it would have killed is left burning with its own staggered countdown, so a car park goes up in a ripple rather than all at once. On the Highwayman, the black filler cap above the left rear wheel is a weak point: shoot it and the car starts to burn.

      At 0% the vehicle becomes a physical wreck, explodes and throws you out. Any non-horse ride, aircraft and boards included, floods when it is 60% under water for half a second, forcing you out, and recovers once it is less than 45% submerged. A flooded vehicle cannot be mounted, and 92% under water for a second and a half while flooded destroys it.

      Flat tyres

      Gunfire and flames burst tyres, and a hard crash or landing can blow one, with the chance rising with the impact.       Dust & washing

Bikes, tanks and the APC stay clean. A car is fully dusty after about a mile and a half of dirt.

      Controls has a Keys tab for rebinding pad buttons per ride (Enter to rebind, Delete to unbind), a Tips tab listing every hint card for the selected ride with a toggle and a reset, and a Records tab for personal bests by ride type.

      Horses is the stable: Make Active, Rename, Remove, and Add ridden horse, which registers the horse you are sitting on when you open the menu.

      Racing holds race setup, the Thunderdome, the Horde challenge and Return Home. Opened during a race it shows a card with your position, lap and time and the choices Restart, Retire, Return Home and Resume, with the world paused behind it.

      Where settings are saved

      Everything lives in the Data config folder. DrivablesNVSE_settings.ini holds the Settings tabs and pad bindings, DrivablesNVSE_workshop.ini holds tuning, DrivablesNVSE_user.ini holds camera trims, DrivablesNVSE_hints.ini holds tip progress and DrivablesHorde.ini holds horde records. Traffic has its own DrivablesTraffic.ini. With Mod Organizer, look in Overwrite.

      What you hear

      Real-world engine recordings, aircraft sounds and effects matched to each ride. These are sound-design choices. The donor’s engine or fuel type does not change the vehicle’s Fallout identity or in-game supply.

      Responds to your driving

      Engine sound follows RPM and load, changing as you accelerate, lift off and shift gear. Profiles can combine separate engine and exhaust recordings, with their own start and shutdown cues.

      The tyre and body sounds add surface rolling, skids, suspension movement, bumps and impacts. Motorcycles also have stunt and landing effects. Human-powered rides keep their own rolling, pedalling or bouncing sounds.

      Beyond engines

      Horns, bells, radio, rotor strain, landing gear, hover thrusters, repulsor pulses, warp boost and parachute wind each have their own role. Horses use hoofbeats, breathing and vocal sounds.

      In F10’s audio settings you can control surface rolling, responsive engine pitch and idle audio. Racing has separate crowd and event-sound controls, and Settings → Showtime controls the music stingers that mark a new ride, a big jump, a take-off, a record and the race start.

      Release & compatibility

      When is it coming out?

      Coming December 2026!

      The public download is forthcoming. Follow development on Oliver Swanick Productions. The mod page is on Nexus Mods.

      Does it need The Frontier?

      No. It has nothing to do with The Frontier and does not need it installed.

      Please don’t use these pages to dunk on it. Criticise my mod if you like, but leave other modders out of it.

      Does it support Tale of Two Wastelands?

      Yes, TTW is supported.

      What about Viva New Vegas?

      New Vegas Drivables has been developed and tested with Viva New Vegas in mind.

      Can I install it during a playthrough?

      Yes. It is safe to install mid-playthrough. You do not need to start a new game.

      Will there be a Fallout 3 or Fallout 4 version?

      No. This is a New Vegas mod. TTW support does not mean a separate Fallout 3 release.

      Playing

      Can I use a controller or Steam Deck?

      Yes. Controllers are supported, and the mod has been tested on Steam Deck.

      Click both sticks together to open the F10 menu. The visual control guide has keyboard and mouse, Xbox and Steam Deck layouts, with separate layouts for different rides.

      What about first person?

      First person has improved, but it will never be my priority.

      Where do I find a vehicle?

      Start with the placement map. It shows where vehicles are, what condition they start in and what you need to claim them. The map switch hides its directions and unlock details. The catalogue and other guides still include spoilers.

      Some work straight away. Others need repairs, a quest completion or a race win. The first ride guide walks you through it.

      Can companions ride too?

      Yes. Suitable vehicles have passenger positions, and eligible humanoid companions can ride horses alongside you. The companion guide lists the seats and explains the restrictions.

      Do I have to keep buying fuel and parts?

      Powered vehicles use the supply listed in their entry. Jerry Cans, cells and Reactor Coolant cover the different types. Cells are ordinary loot. Jerry Cans come from four traders who stock one at a time, plus a dozen left lying around the Mojave that never come back. Reactor Coolant works the same way with two traders, Knight Torres and the Boomers’ munitions manager, and fourteen loose cans, one beside each dead reactor vehicle and the rest at reactor sites, plus the odd can in a toolbox or a pile of factory junk. The Alien Recon Craft and human-powered rides need no fuel.

      Required repair tools and components are consumed when used. See fuel and repairs for the details.

      My vehicle is stuck. What now?

      Press T to recover a stuck vehicle, or use Recover in the F10 garage. On a controller, hold View / Back for 0.7 seconds. Tapping that controller button changes the camera instead. These are the default bindings on rides that support recovery.

      Recover works while you are aboard, stopped and settled on the ground, with a short cooldown. Aircraft cannot use it, a boat reverses off the bank instead, and in a race it only rights an overturned car. If you were thrown clear, a marker floats over the ride until you get back to it.

      Can I fast travel or save while driving?

      Yes to both. Fast travel from the Pip-Boy map while riding and you arrive still in the seat, and a ridden horse comes along. A save made while riding loads you straight back into the seat with the engine running. Parked vehicles stay where you left them.

      Can I shoot from a vehicle?

      Pistols from cars, bikes and the oddballs, and pistols, rifles and one-handed melee from a horse. The tanks and aircraft have their own guns, missiles, a flamethrower, a bomb bay and a tractor beam. The combat guide has the rules for each.

      Can I skip the kill cam without getting out?

      Yes. Activate ends the kill cam as it always did, and while the cam is running that press never counts as leaving your ride.

      Is there traffic?

      Yes. After Goodsprings, other riders appear on the roads near you, mostly cyclists with the odd horse and novelty ride. You can rob a cyclist for their bike, and hostiles can take a ride you leave lying around. On the road covers it, along with trailers, pedestrians and props that break.

      Make it your own

      Press F10, or click both controller sticks together. These options are under Settings → Loading. Apply your changes before leaving the menu.

      “I don’t like the loading wheels.”

      Set Loading wheel to Off. The change takes effect when a fresh loading screen opens. It leaves whichever loading spinner you otherwise have installed.

      “I don’t like the main menu replacer.”

      Set Main menu background to Off, apply it, then restart the game. It takes effect from the next launch.

      “I don’t want vehicle loading screens either.”

      Set Loading screens to Off. This removes the mod’s studio shots and driving tips from the rotation, leaving the game’s and other mods’ screens.

      Prefer to change the configuration file?

      With the game closed, edit Data\config\DrivablesNVSE_settings.ini in your active mod setup. These are saved overrides. Add or update the relevant entries under the existing [Display] section, or create that section if it is missing.

      [Display]
bLoadingWheel=0
bMainMenuBackground=0
bLoadingScreens=0

      Use only the lines for the features you want to turn off. Set a value to 1 to enable it again. If you use Mod Organizer, the saved settings file may be in Overwrite.

      “The camera moves too much.”

      F10 has Full, Reduced and Off camera motion options, plus settings for speed FOV, road shake, camera tilt and jump slow motion. Start by reducing camera motion.

      “I don’t want stunt XP.”

      Set Stunt XP to Off under F10 → Settings → Showtime.

      “I don’t want dust on my car.”

      Set Dust to Off under Settings → Bodywork. Rain and the Wash option on the service wheel clean an existing coat.

      “I don’t want doors and bonnets falling off.”

      Set Deformation and destruction to Off under Settings → Bodywork. Condition damage still applies, nothing visible breaks. Repair clears any damage already there.

      “Stop people reacting to my horn.”

      Set Horn NPC reactions to Off under Settings → Driving. The horn itself still works.

      “I’ll switch my own headlights on, thanks.”

      Set Automatic headlights to Off under Settings → Driving.

      “No music stingers, no camera tricks.”

      Under Settings → Showtime, set Stingers to Off and Race fly-by to Off. Under Settings → Camera, set Crash director to Off, and under Settings → Effects set Missile camera chance to 0.

      “I don’t want traffic.”

      With the game closed, set bTraffic=0 in Data\config\DrivablesTraffic.ini. There is no F10 switch for it.

      “Where did my settings go?”

      Data\config\DrivablesNVSE_settings.ini holds the Settings tabs and pad bindings, DrivablesNVSE_workshop.ini holds tuning, DrivablesNVSE_user.ini holds camera trims and DrivablesNVSE_hints.ini holds tip progress. The F10 menu guide lists what each tab contains.
