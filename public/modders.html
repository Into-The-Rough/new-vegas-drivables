Your first car

The current template is drivables:road-car-v1, for a wheeled car with one physical body. You supply a NIF, an ESP and an INI pack.

  1. Make the model

    Author the body, wheel controls and collision in your NIF. Measure the axle positions, wheel sizes, seat and camera points from that model.

  2. Create the vehicle record

    In your own ESP, add a CONT record and point its model path at your NIF. Use IDLE records for driver poses. Detached parts usually use MSTT records.

  3. Describe it in a pack

    Create pack.ini and a vehicle definition under config/DrivablesNVSE/addons/. Give the pack and vehicle stable names. Fill every measured field in the template.

  4. Fit the driver and tune it

    Choose a driver idle or animation pack, fit the seat and check both cameras. Set mass, engine torque, braking and gear ratios. Keep optional damage disabled until its parts are ready.

  5. Validate, package, then test

    The author kit checks files and definitions. Check driving, collision, entry, exit and saving in game yourself. Package the ESP, models, textures and definitions as a separate mod.

A pack owns its files

MyCar/
  authoring.json
  Data/
    YourCars.esp
    meshes/yourname/coupe.nif
    textures/yourname/...
    config/DrivablesNVSE/addons/
      yourname.cars/
        pack.ini
        vehicles/coupe.ini
        parts/body.ini

parts/body.ini is optional. authoring.json lists the assets to package. It does not generate your model.

Keep your definitions in the add-on folder. You do not need to edit the main vehicle INI.

sPrompt=Drive Coupe
sDriverIdleID=YourCars.esp|001235

This is the identity portion only. Complete the geometry and drivetrain fields in the downloaded template.

001234 is a local FormID. Leave out the load-order prefix. Keep yourname.cars:coupe unchanged when updating a released pack.

Using the author kit

From the extracted author kit, create a project, fill in its files and then run validation and packaging. These commands are for a terminal, not the game console.

python tools\drivables_author.py new --template road-car-v1 --id yourname.cars --output MyCar
python tools\drivables_author.py validate --pack MyCar --data "PATH_TO_RESOLVED_DATA"
python tools\drivables_author.py package --pack MyCar --data "PATH_TO_RESOLVED_DATA" --output MyCar.zip

Replace PATH_TO_RESOLVED_DATA with the Data directory containing the dependencies the game sees. Install the kit’s Python requirements first. PASS means the offline checks passed. ERROR and INCOMPLETE block packaging.

Draft download note: the full kit is not bundled on this page yet. Its red and blue example cars have offline checks, but still need their first in-game sign-off. The optional exporter creates simple examples from dimensions. A general Blender scene exporter is not included.

Build the rig before the details

Use the starter hierarchy as a reference. The body and wheels share one vehicle, with one rigid body providing collision.

Vehicle root · BSFadeNode
├─ collision · one rigid body
└─ Bip01
   └─ Base
      └─ RotControl
         ├─ Body
         │  ├─ body geometry
         │  └─ DriverSeat
         ├─ SteerFL
         │  └─ WheelFL
         ├─ SteerFR
         │  └─ WheelFR
         ├─ WheelRL
         └─ WheelRR

Measure, don’t guess

The template asks for front and rear axle distances, half-track, wheel radii and widths, suspension positions, contact probes, seat offsets and camera fit.

The required controls include Base, RotControl, both front steering nodes and all four wheel nodes. The model also needs usable rigid-body mass and valid transforms.

Check the exported model, including its collision. A shape that looks correct in the viewport can still have the wrong origin, winding or pivot.

This tree follows the simple author-kit example. The numbered DeSoto picture is a component index, not a dump of its NIF hierarchy.

01 / Rolling gear

Wheels & tyres

Front wheels spin under their steering controls. Put the steering pivot at the wheel centre and keep the wheel geometry under its spin node.

  • Measure front and rear radius and half-width separately.
  • Match axle positions and suspension offsets to the exported rig.
  • Check spin direction, steering and ground contact in game.
  • After mirroring a wheel, check outward normals and triangle winding.
SteerFLsteering→WheelFLrotation→Tyre meshappearance

Puncture behaviour is enabled with bTyrePop. Test it with DrvPopTyre. A convincing flat tyre needs a compatible deformation rig, beyond a spinning wheel.

A worked tyre-deformation example is still needed for the public kit.

02 / Windows

Glass

Keep breakable panes in named geometry shapes. Declare those shapes and their damage groups in [Glass local-id] definitions, linked through your parts set.

  • Leave the opaque window frames outside the shattering shapes.
  • Check transparency from inside and outside the car.
  • Enable bGlassShatter only when the definitions and shapes exist.
  • Test damage, repair and reload together.

The current parts set supports up to sixteen glass shapes. Its names must resolve to geometry in the loaded model.

03 / Seeing the road

Headlights

A lamp lens, a visible beam and light falling on the world are separate things. Check each one when authoring the front lamps.

  • HeadlightL and HeadlightR locate the front lights.
  • The fleet uses beam geometry named HeadlightConeL and HeadlightConeR.
  • Fit the anchors to the model and check aim after steering, pitch and roll.
  • Check the lights both parked and while driving.

The dedicated front-light recipe still needs an add-on example covering lens materials, beam orientation and profile settings. These node names describe the existing implementation.

04 / Bodywork

Doors, bonnet, boot & bumpers

A detachable part needs an attached control node, a separate debris model and a matching prop record. Its definition supplies the node, prop, marker and measured offsets or bounds.

  • Most detached props use MSTT records. A bonnet may explicitly use CONT.
  • bHoodDamage covers bonnet and boot parts. Doors and bumpers have separate gates.
  • Fit entry and exit animations to the doors you actually authored.
  • Check the part after damage, repair and save/load.

Per parts set: one bonnet, one boot, two doors and two bumpers. The blue author-kit example demonstrates a detachable bonnet.

05 / Rear lamps

Braking, reversing & damage

Declare the brake and reverse parent nodes, their glow shapes, the damaged shapes and hit bounds in [RearLamp local-id].

Brakebrake glow parentReversereverse glow parentBrokendamaged shapes

The glow must be beneath the parent which switches it. Merely giving a shape the right name is not enough. Rear-lamp damage uses the glass-shatter gate.

Two lamp slots are supported. Number them from zero without gaps. Test brake and reverse separately, then shoot and repair each lamp.

06 / People

Driver & passengers

Fit hands, feet and seating height to your model. An existing animation pack provides motion, but its pose will still need checking against a new steering wheel and seat.

  • Author the driver anchor and first-person camera separately.
  • Allow room for a standing exit beside the car.
  • Declare passenger positions explicitly. Their count excludes the driver.
  • Check entry and exit from both sides where supported.

Named driver packs bind through sDriverPack. Entry and exit definitions bind through sTransitionPack. Custom transitions include durations and seat-transfer timing.

Give it its own character

Once the rig works, tune the parts the player will feel and hear.

Handling

Start with mass, wheel geometry, engine torque and brake torque. Set a positive gear ratio for every active gear, decreasing as the gears rise. Check acceleration, turning, braking and rough ground before chasing a top-speed number.

Sound

Use [AudioProfile local-id] for RPM banks and cues, bound with sAudioProfile. Fit acceleration and coasting recordings, then ignition and other one-shots. Impact banks have their own material classes and tap, hit and break folders.

Fuel & damage

The blank template starts with fuel and boost disabled. Decide which systems suit the vehicle, then configure and test them. Keep the player’s service transaction separate from direct script repairs and fuel changes.

Effects & variants

Add liveries, exhaust and a wreck only where the matching resources and configuration exist. Some fleet-specific effects and body dent meshes still use built-in definitions. They need their own add-on support before being promised as generic features.

Command reference

63 registered commands, grouped by what they do. Expand a row for syntax and return values. Reserved, removed command slots are omitted.

Calling reference

MyCarRef.DrvGetVehicleSpeed

The reference before the dot is the vehicle being queried. In the console, selecting that vehicle supplies the calling reference.

Explicit argument

DrvSetFuel 10 MyCarRef

The older service commands accept an explicit vehicle after the value. Follow each signature. Square brackets mean an optional argument.

Examples use placeholder editor IDs such as MyCarRef. Replace them with your own records or script variables. In the console, select a placed reference or use its resolved FormID. A base form defines a vehicle type. A reference is one particular vehicle in the world.

React to a ride

Use events for changes such as mounting or wrecking. These six events are registered with xNVSE. Handlers are flushed on load, so register them again when your game-load initialisation runs.

EventArguments, in orderUse
Drv:OnMountvehicle, actorReact when a rider mounts.
Drv:OnDismountvehicle, actorReact when a rider dismounts.
Drv:OnVehicleWreckedvehicleHandle the loss of a vehicle.
Drv:OnRideEndvehicle, driver, reasonDistinguish arrival from an interrupted NPC ride.
Drv:OnEncounterSpawnquest, leaderTrack the newly started encounter.
Drv:OnEncounterEndquestHandle the end of an encounter.

Ride-end reasons: 0 arrived, 1 aborted, 2 stopped, 3 driver lost, 4 wrecked, 5 handover, 6 world reset, 7 robbed.

Check that a returned reference still exists before acting on it. An accepted command can start a transition or queue work. Use the appropriate state query or event to follow the result.

A few useful starting points

These snippets belong inside your own script. Declare the variables you use and replace the example records with your own.

Check a player’s ride

let vehicle := Player.DrvGetActorVehicle
if vehicle
    let condition := vehicle.DrvGetVehicleCondition
    let passengers := vehicle.DrvGetPassengerCount
endif

This also finds the vehicle when the actor is a passenger. The driver is queried separately.

Unlock a placed quest reward

DrvSetVehicleLocked 0 MyRewardCarRef

Run from your quest’s reward logic. This clears the plugin lock. Ownership, keys, faults and other access rules still apply. A placed reference gives you a fixed parking spot.

Set up a restoration

DrvSetVehicleFault "The engine needs a replacement part." MyCarRef
DrvSetVehicleRestoration MyRepairPart 50 MyCarRef

Run setup once. MyRepairPart must be a MISC item. Later, DrvRestoreVehicle attempts the repair using the player’s inventory and Repair skill.

Give a horse a command

let horse := DrvGetActiveHorse
if horse
    horse.DrvSetHorseCommand 2
endif

Command 2 means wait. It requires a living stabled horse. Use 1 for follow or 0 for free.

When it doesn’t work

Start with the validator’s file, section and field message. For runtime rejection, check drivables_nvse.log in the game directory or your mod manager’s overwrite output.

The pack is rejected

Check required plugins, qualified FormIDs, record types, duplicate ownership and missing files. Two packs cannot claim the same vehicle base or livery. Unknown definition keys are errors. INCOMPLETE means a check could not run, not that the pack passed.

The car appears, but I cannot mount it

Check the loaded rigid body, mass, required control nodes and transforms. Then check any declared part nodes, debris models and driver animations. The log includes the vehicle ID and rejection reason. DrvGetVehicleUseFailure can identify a gameplay access restriction.

The wheels float, turn strangely or shade incorrectly

Compare the exported wheel centres and radii with the INI measurements. Check steering and spin pivots, front/rear offsets and contact probes. For a mirrored wheel, inspect triangle winding and outward normals before changing its textures or specular settings.

A light or damage feature does nothing

Check that the feature is enabled, its parts set is bound and its exact node or shape names exist. For rear lamps, confirm that each glow is a child of its switched parent. Test with a fresh model load after changing the definitions.

I changed the files, but the old setup remains

Restart the game after changing manifests, forms, models, feature definitions or animations. Use the workshop for supported live numeric tuning. Check which copy of the file wins in your mod manager.

Before sharing your vehicle

Keep the pack’s stable IDs and dependency declarations intact. Package only the files your add-on needs, with its asset credits.

  • Enter, drive, reverse, brake and exit.
  • Check collision and wheel contact on uneven ground.
  • Check the driver, passengers and both cameras.
  • Damage and repair every optional component.
  • Save, reload and return after a cell change.
  • Install the packaged ZIP in a clean mod-manager profile.

Draft scope: road cars have the current author template. Aircraft, boats, bikes and living mounts need their own converted templates and examples. Some model-specific effects still need to move into pack definitions.
